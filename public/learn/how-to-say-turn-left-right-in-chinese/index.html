How to Say "Turn Left / Turn Right" in Real Chinese

Your textbook probably taught 向左转 (xiàng zuǒ zhuǎn) for "turn left." It's not wrong — but ask a local for directions and you'll hear 往左拐 (wǎng zuǒ guǎi) instead. Here's the pattern that actually gets used on the street.

The real pattern: 往 + direction + 拐

往 (wǎng, "toward") + a direction + 拐 (guǎi, "to turn") is how people tell you which way to go.

ChinesePinyinMeaning
往左拐wǎng zuǒ guǎiturn left
往右拐wǎng yòu guǎiturn right
往前走wǎng qián zǒugo straight / keep going

拐 literally means "to turn a corner," so 往左拐 is like "hook left." You'll also hear 拐弯 (guǎiwān), "to make a turn."

Textbook vs. spoken: 转 or 拐?

Turn left
Textbook向左转 (xiàng zuǒ zhuǎn)
Spoken往左拐 (wǎng zuǒ guǎi)

The real switch is 转 → 拐. 向左转 sounds stiff — it's close to the military drill command for "left face." For everyday directions, 拐 sounds far more natural. (向左拐 with 向 is fine too; the word people actually change is 转 to 拐.)

Put it together

  • 一直走 (yìzhí zǒu) — go straight ahead
  • 到红绿灯往右拐 (dào hónglǜdēng wǎng yòu guǎi) — at the traffic light, turn right
  • 到前面路口往左拐 (dào qiánmiàn lùkǒu wǎng zuǒ guǎi) — at the intersection ahead, turn left
The shortcut — for directions, say 往 + 左/右 + 拐, and 往前走 for straight. Save 向…转 for the textbook.
