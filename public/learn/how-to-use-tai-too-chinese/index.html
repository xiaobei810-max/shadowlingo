How to Use 太 (tài) — "Too Sweet", "Too Sour", Too Everything

China's most controversial soda isn't a fancy flavor — it's 香醋可乐 (xiāngcù kělè), Coke with black vinegar. Try it and your first reaction in Chinese is automatic: 太酸了！(tài suān le!) — "too sour!" That one word, 太, is one of the most useful building blocks in Chinese.

The pattern: 太 + adjective (+ 了)

太 (tài) means "too" — as in "excessively." Put it in front of any adjective and you've got a complete, natural-sounding reaction. Adding 了 (le) at the end softens it slightly and makes it sound more colloquial — you'll hear it constantly.

ChinesePinyinMeaning
太甜了tài tián letoo sweet
太酸了tài suān letoo sour
太辣了tài là letoo spicy
太贵了tài guì letoo expensive
太好了tài hǎo legreat! / too good
太可爱了tài kě'ài leso cute!

Not just complaints — 太 works for praise too

太好了！is one of the most common expressions of delight in Chinese — "great!", "perfect!", "that's wonderful!" 太厉害了！(tài lìhai le!) means "that's amazing / you're incredible." The same structure works for any feeling, positive or negative.

Real examples

  • 这个太甜了，我不太喜欢。(zhège tài tián le, wǒ bú tài xǐhuān.) — "This is too sweet, I don't really like it."
  • 太酸了！你确定这是可乐？(tài suān le! nǐ quèdìng zhè shì kělè?) — "Too sour! Are you sure this is Coke?"
  • 太贵了，能便宜一点吗？(tài guì le, néng piányí yīdiǎn ma?) — "Too expensive — can you go a bit lower?"
  • 太好了，就这个！(tài hǎo le, jiù zhège!) — "Perfect, this one!"
The shortcut — 太 + adjective + 了 is your instant reaction to anything. Too sweet, too spicy, too expensive, too good — same pattern every time.
