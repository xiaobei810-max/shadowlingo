What Does 上火 (Shàng Huǒ) Mean? The Chinese Word With No English Translation

Ask a Chinese friend why they've got a sore throat and there's a good chance they'll say 我上火了 (wǒ shàng huǒ le). Try to translate that into English and you'll come up short — because there isn't a word for it. 上火 is one of those concepts English simply doesn't carry.

Breaking down the word

CharacterPinyinMeaning
上shàngto rise, to go up
火huǒfire

Put them together and you get "fire rising" — too much inner heat in your body. Not a fever, not an infection. It's a state of imbalance that Chinese people connect to a specific cluster of symptoms.

What counts as 上火

If you've got any of these, someone will tell you you're 上火:

  • 嗓子疼 (sǎngzi téng) — sore throat
  • 口腔溃疡 (kǒuqiāng kuìyáng) — mouth ulcers
  • 长痘 (zhǎng dòu) — breaking out
  • 便秘 (biànmì) — constipation
  • 流鼻血 (liú bíxuè) — nosebleeds

What causes it — and how to bring it down

The usual suspects: 辣的 (là de) spicy food, 油炸的 (yóuzhá de) fried food, 熬夜 (áoyè) staying up late. The fix is to 降火 (jiàng huǒ) — literally "lower the fire."

That's where 凉茶 (liáng chá) comes in: "cool tea," a category of herbal drinks made for exactly this. You'll find them in every convenience store. 金银花露 (jīnyínhuā lù), honeysuckle, is one of the most common — light, faintly sweet, and specifically marketed as a 降火 drink.

ExpressionPinyinMeaning
我上火了wǒ shàng huǒ leI've got inner heat
降火jiàng huǒbring the heat down
凉茶liáng chácooling herbal drink
去火qù huǒget rid of the heat (same idea)
这个很上火zhège hěn shàng huǒthis food causes inner heat
The shortcut — 上火 = too much inner heat, blamed on spicy food, fried food and late nights. The cure is to 降火, usually with a bottle of 凉茶. No English equivalent, so don't translate it — just use it.
