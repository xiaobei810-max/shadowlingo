What Is 胖大海 (Pàng Dà Hǎi)? The Chinese Drink Called "Fat Big Sea"

Walk into a Chinese convenience store and you'll find a bottle with three big characters on it: 胖大海 (pàng dà hǎi). Translate them one by one and you get "fat big sea." There's nothing from the ocean inside. Here's what's actually going on.

Breaking down the name

CharacterPinyinMeaning
胖pàngfat, plump
大dàbig
海hǎisea

胖大海 is a dried seed — small, wrinkled, unremarkable. Drop it into hot water and it swells to many times its original size, turning soft and spongy until it fills the cup. The name describes what it does, not what it is: it gets fat, it gets big. Chinese product names often work this way.

Why people drink it

胖大海 is a traditional remedy for a sore throat (嗓子疼, sǎngzi téng) or a worn-out voice. Teachers, singers, and anyone who talks all day keep it around. Bottled versions are usually blended with other herbs — 罗汉果 (luóhànguǒ, monk fruit), 金银花 (jīnyínhuā, honeysuckle), 胎菊 (tāijú, young chrysanthemum) — and sold as 0糖 zero sugar, 0脂 zero fat.

The 胖 you'll actually use

That same 胖 shows up constantly in everyday Chinese — and unlike "fat" in English, it's not automatically rude. People say it about themselves all the time:

  • 我胖了 (wǒ pàng le) — "I've gained weight."
  • 他有点胖 (tā yǒudiǎn pàng) — "He's a little heavy."
  • 胖子 (pàngzi) — a heavyset person (casual; can be affectionate between friends, rude from a stranger)
The shortcut — when a Chinese name sounds impossible translated word by word, ask what the thing does. 胖大海 doesn't come from the sea; it just gets fat and big in your cup.
