摇一摇 — Why Chinese Grass Jelly Drinks Tell You to Shake First

Pick up a bottle of 烧仙草 (shāo xiāncǎo) — grass jelly drink — in China and it usually has a label or sticker that says 摇一摇 (yáo yi yáo): "give it a shake." Skip this step and you're drinking plain sugar water for the first half, because all the jelly has sunk to the bottom. That little phrase is also a perfect example of one of Chinese's most useful grammar patterns.

摇一摇 = "give it a little shake"

摇 (yáo) means "to shake." The pattern verb＋一＋verb softens the action — it means doing something briefly, casually, just a little. 摇一摇 = "give it a shake." It's lighter and more natural than just saying 摇摇 (which also works) or the flat command 摇 (shake).

ExpressionPinyinMeaning
摇一摇yáo yi yáogive it a shake
试一试shì yi shìgive it a try
尝一尝cháng yi chánghave a taste
看一看kàn yi kàntake a look
等一等děng yi děngwait a moment

What is 烧仙草?

烧仙草 (shāo xiāncǎo) — literally "burned immortal grass" — is a popular drink made from grass jelly (xiāncǎo 仙草), a plant that sets into a dark, slightly bitter jelly. It's sold everywhere in Taiwan and mainland China, usually mixed with milk tea, coconut water, or syrup. The jelly sinks, hence the instruction to 摇一摇 before drinking.

Real examples of verb一verb

  • 你先尝一尝，好吃再买。(nǐ xiān cháng yi cháng, hào chī zài mǎi.) — "Try it first, then buy if you like it."
  • 等一等，我马上来。(děng yi děng, wǒ mǎshàng lái.) — "Hold on, I'll be right there."
  • 这个你可以试一试。(zhège nǐ kěyǐ shì yi shì.) — "You can give this a try."
  • 摇一摇再喝！(yáo yi yáo zài hē!) — "Shake it first, then drink!"
The shortcut — verb＋一＋verb means "do it briefly / give it a go." 摇一摇, 试一试, 看一看 — same pattern, endlessly reusable.
